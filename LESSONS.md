## 2026-10-25: Brian Mark, "NEW Instagram Algorithm Update Favours Carousels" (videos/2026-10-25_brianmark_carousels.mp4, Therm's screen recording of the Reel)
Learned by: Brain, Researcher and Designer (Therm sent it on Oct 25 2026). 10 seconds, watched frame by frame (2 per second) with the burned-in captions. Structure only, never his wording.

- Hook: one sticky headline in a white box at the top for the whole Reel ("algorithm update favours carousels"), talking head below. The claim is his; we never repeat it as fact.
- Format and beats: the same sentence three times, each with a different wall of carousel examples behind it:
  1. "Post carousels like these and you'll build connection": STORY carousels. Each slide is a still of the creator (a paused-video look) with ONE short subtitle line in white sentence case, low and centered ("My mom kicked me out", "when I was 8 years old..."). Cliffhanger ellipses pull the swipe.
  2. "...you'll warm your audience up": VALUE / HOW-TO carousels. Cover = photo of the creator, huge condensed all-caps headline, one line in yellow, a 3D icon above it, "Swipe to learn how ->". Inside = an orange step label ("MONDAY AM:"), a bold white title, and a REAL screenshot on a white card with a red hand-drawn circle around the exact button or number, sometimes a curved arrow. Page counter top right.
  3. "...your audience will want to buy from you": PROOF carousels. A white card like a screenshot (name in caps, a one-line setup, a picture, a punchline) and a dark slide with big numbers line by line plus a "results are not typical" disclaimer.
  4. "Carousels are the top performing format" + comment-keyword offer.
- On-screen text: subtitle-style captions, heavy condensed caps with one yellow line, orange step labels, red marker circles.
- Use for us (built into formats.py, see PLAYBOOK "Carousel jobs"):
  1. "style": "story" = CONNECTION. Therm's own photos only, one Verified Story Bank fact per slide, subtitle style. Pairs with hooks 19 to 24.
  2. "style": "bold" = WARM-UP. Condensed headline cover on Therm's photo + "Swipe to learn how"; "step" slides with a real screenshot of the OFFICIAL source page (tools/shoot.py through the "Screenshot official pages" robot) circled in red. The screenshot doubles as the source proof.
  3. "style": "proof" = BUY. Only the story-bank client win (Burlington County, DPA, $1,800 back at closing), no names, no client photo (illustration only), disclaimer "Results vary. Every buyer's situation is different." and "Talk to a licensed lender for your numbers."
  4. Run all three jobs every week, so the feed builds trust, teaches, then shows proof.
- Do not copy: his captions or headlines, "comment GAME" or any comment keyword (our only CTA is "Click the link in my bio."), his income claims or client results, his photos, third-party profile screenshots, faith lines from his story slides, or the "algorithm update" claim (unverified).

## 2026-10-22: Alex Hormozi, "My Actual Social Media Strategy For 2027 - The Algorithm" (https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ZTSI3DDP_4A)
Learned by: Researcher. Structure only, never his wording. Watched via the video's own captions (16:01 long) plus a frame check; Therm asked for it on Oct 22 2026.

- Hook: opens on a tension between two opposite camps (make it perfect vs. make a lot) and says the answer sits in the middle (0:00 to 0:20). No intro, no "hey guys". The tension is stated in the first sentence.
- Format and beats: one long raw talking-head take, shot on location on a phone-style camera, no color grade, burned-in captions. Beats:
  1. Tension between two extremes, promise of the middle path (0:00).
  2. A named 5-step process, walked one step at a time, each step with one concrete example from his own team: question every requirement, delete what doesn't matter, simplify (most result for least effort), speed up the timeline, and only then automate (1:00 to 10:10).
  3. Shift to the big idea: people now want to be SHOWN, not told. Content that teaches a specific customer to solve a specific problem is the content that brings in business (10:10 to 11:30).
  4. Two conditions for people to act on your advice: the steps must be easy to understand, and they must believe it will pay off. Proof is what creates that belief (11:30 to 12:15).
  5. A short story of someone hiding a weak spot, then the fix: tell the whole truth, including where you looked bad, and people believe the good parts (12:55 to 14:57).
  6. Stay in your lane: only teach where you have proof (14:57 to 15:17).
  7. Free resource offer as the close (15:17).
- On-screen text: burned-in captions only. No graphics, titles or b-roll. The message carries it, not the production.
- Strategy points (his 2027 approach, in our words):
  - Quality volume: keep the substance high and cut polish that does not move views, follows or leads.
  - Education wins: teach a specific buyer to solve a specific problem, step by step.
  - Proof is the product: real results beat claims.
  - The whole truth sells: show the mistake, then the lesson.
  - Only speak where you have proof.
  - Before automating any step, ask if the step should exist at all.
- Use for us (NJ first-time-buyer carousels):
  1. Tension cover: open with two opposite camps a first-time buyer hears (for example "wait for prices to drop" vs. "buy now") and promise the middle answer on slide 2. Pair with hooks from the Contrarian or Curiosity family.
  2. Numbered process carousel: one named step per slide, each with one concrete NJ example (for example the steps from pre-approval to keys, using the corrected timeline facts). This is our "Real Estate 101" Slot A.
  3. "Whole truth" story carousel: mistake first, lesson second, using ONLY the Verified Story Bank (rented 10+ years and paid $150K+ in rent; waited too long on online rent payments at the 2-family). Pairs with hooks 19 to 24.
  4. Proof slide: use the verified client win (Burlington County buyer, down payment help, $1,800 back at closing) as proof on DPA-related posts, privacy-safe, ending with "Talk to a licensed lender for your numbers."
  5. Each content slide gives one action a buyer can take, in plain words (lower the effort to understand), and the proof slide raises belief it works.
  6. Stay in the Realtor lane; no loan mechanics (same as his "only where you have proof" point).
  7. Production check: before adding any design extra, ask "does this get more follows or DMs?" If not, cut it.
- Do not copy: his phrases, his invented word for the quality-plus-volume idea, the book and its 5-step names as a branded framework, his stories, his profanity, his roadmap offer. Our only call to action stays "Click the link in my bio."
