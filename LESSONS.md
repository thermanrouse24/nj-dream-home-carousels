## 2026-10-23: Alex Hormozi, "My Actual Social Media Strategy For 2027" (https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ZTSI3DDP_4A)
Learned by: Researcher. Evidence: the video's own English auto-captions (16:02). Video frames could not be downloaded (403), so nothing here is about how it looks.
- Hook: the first 30 seconds set up two camps that disagree (put out a lot vs. make each post great), say the answer sits between them, then promise a step-by-step method from an outside source (0:00-0:45).
- Format and beats: one long talking-head take, filmed raw on purpose. Beats: (1) a numbered 5-step method, each step shown with one real example from his own team (1:00-10:00); (2) a test for each step: did it bring more views, leads or money? (9:00); (3) the content idea for this year: teach and show proof, don't just make claims (10:00-12:30); (4) a story where someone asks him a question, he asks a few questions back, and the real answer comes out (13:00-14:30); (5) tell the whole story, the bad parts too, and stay on topics you can prove (14:30-15:20); (6) one free resource as the call to action (15:20-16:02).
- Strategy in one line: spend the effort on what is said, not on polish, and post more often; teaching backed by proof is what turns views into clients; the bad parts are what make the good parts believable.
- On-screen text: unknown (no frames).
- Use for us:
  1. Cover idea: "two camps" tension on a first-time-buyer topic (for example savers vs. buy-now people), with the swipe giving a sourced middle answer.
  2. Myth-check carousel: one "rule" buyers believe per slide (20% down, perfect credit, etc.), ask it three questions on the slide (what does it really mean, where does that come from, does it matter), then give the sourced NJ fact. Facts still come from the Researcher's sources, never from this video.
  3. Proof over claims: each teaching slide carries a real number or source, plus a real result of Therm's when there is one (with the client's OK).
  4. Show the downside: give one slide to the real cost or catch (closing costs, PMI, inspection surprises) so the benefit slides are believable.
  5. Story carousel: a real buyer question, back-and-forth questions across slides 2-4, the answer on slide 5, CTA on slide 6.
  6. Stay in Therm's lane (buying a home in NJ); don't give tax, legal or lending advice beyond sourced facts.
  7. End on one free, specific resource or next step (within the playbook's CTA rules).
- Do not copy: his words, catchphrases or made-up terms, the book he cites, his stories and examples, the swearing, his free-resource offer, or any claim or number from the video.
